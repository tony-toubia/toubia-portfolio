# Tiny Turbo Club

Three original courses (Cloudtop Circuit, Peachy Palms, Twilight Trail), three free original toy cars, three local computer opponents and two-lap races. Standalone route: `/tiny-turbo`; portfolio Projects entry included. No advertising, payments, accounts, online players, or external asset requests. The existing bundled Three.js runtime is reused.

Automatic acceleration and track-following keep steering manageable for young children. A floating joystick accepts a thumb anywhere on the left side; a small dead zone and analog strength allow gentle steering, while the base follows an extended thumb for quick reversal, like the map mobile game. The right-side boost works independently. Release, pointer cancellation, pause, questions, resize and backgrounding clear steering. Arrow keys or A/D remain available. Gentle recentering is optional. Grass and barriers slow the car without crashes or elimination. All power-ups help the player: rainbow speed, a star magnet, and a bubble that preserves speed on grass. No weapons or attack items.

Learning defaults to Kindergarten, mixed math/spelling and a stop every 18 seconds of actual racing. Grades K–5, math-only/spelling-only/mixed and 12/18/26-second cadences are selectable. This is a starter question generator and spelling bank, not a curriculum or automatic assessment. K arithmetic stays within 0–5; later levels extend to 20/100, multiplication/division and simple equivalent fractions. Spelling includes a picture/clue and optional browser text-to-speech. Wrong answers allow another try with a hint. Questions have no timer; every racer freezes. Correct answers give four stars and one boost; skipping resumes without the bonus or penalty.

Finish a race to bank collected stars plus a finish bonus. Twelve stars buys one speed upgrade, up to three per car. All cars and courses are available immediately. Profile/preferences, garage progress, course selection and separate course best times are kept in localStorage, with in-memory play if storage is unavailable. Old saves keep their stars and upgrades. Best times exclude learning/pause time and update once per finish. No child name, date of birth or other personal data is requested. Leaving a tab pauses active racing; abandon-to-garage does not bank unfinished race stars.

## Models and expansion

The initial cars are original procedural meshes, not Hot Wheels replicas. `CARS` in `public/tiny-turbo/core.mjs` is the catalog; `makeCar` builds its three reusable body styles. A future glTF adapter can supply an optional asset URL, scale and wheel metadata while keeping car IDs/progress stable. Check each model's license and attribution before adding community cars. A ready-to-use reusable official Hot Wheels game model library was not verified. Candidate search results are not approval to redistribute branded assets.

`TRACKS` in `core.mjs` defines the course catalog, control points and palettes; `world.mjs` builds the shared road, power-ups and scenery. Cloudtop has balloons and soft foliage, Peachy Palms has water, palms and umbrellas, and Twilight Trail has pines, glowing mushrooms, stars and moonlight. Routes have distinct closed splines. Scenery uses instanced geometry, and switching courses releases old geometry, materials and textures. The lobby includes actual route previews, a distinct night palette, a clear course/car/start flow and responsive controls. Next priorities: real-device iPad feel/performance testing, more spelling vocabulary and question shapes, additional drivers and local same-device profiles. Multiplayer is outside the first version.

## Checks

`npm run tiny-turbo:test` checks 3,600 generated questions, valid answer choices, kindergarten limits, race clock freezing, skip/retry behavior, one-time finish rewards, upgrade spending/caps and saved-data normalization. The gameplay harness executes the real module with actual Three.js geometry plus a minimal DOM/renderer adapter. It covers joystick direction through the chase camera, analog strength, reversal, multi-pointer ownership, simultaneous boost, cancellation/pause cleanup, all three course finishes/records, course disposal, replay, upgrades and settings. Route checks cover closure, island bounds and clearance between separated road sections. The harness does not render WebGL pixels; visual browser and physical iPad/touchscreen testing are separate checks.

Browser checks on October 24, 2026 covered all three rendered worlds, car/course selection, joystick drag/release, boost, pause/garage, and a wrong-answer retry followed by a correct answer and reward. Layouts were inspected at 1280×720, 390×844, 844×390, 768×1024 and 1024×768. Portrait tablet inspection caught an off-screen garage preview; its camera framing now keeps the car visible. Physical iPad multi-touch feel and sustained performance still need device testing.
