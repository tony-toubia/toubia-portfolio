## Articulation design

The importer now retains distinct source assembly instances, including repeated instances of the same submodel. Older packed models recover this metadata only when source physical-part traversal exactly matches the installed model. This backfill changes assembly metadata and motion profiles; geometry, placement matrices, inventory, colors and building steps are preserved.

`scripts/studforge/detect-motion.mjs` runs automatically during conversion. Shared rules recognize 3680/3679 turntables, 3937/3938/6134 tilt hinges, 2429/2430 plate hinges and 3831/3830 brick hinges. `connector-reader.mjs` reads official LDraw finger/click-hinge macros and Technic circular/keyed-hole macros through transformed subparts; standard Technic bricks supply their official hole grids. Straight axles and common pure pins use their actual local shaft axis and extent. Keyed holes join rigidly to axles; circular bearings form rotational boundaries. This is per-part/primitive support reused across all kits, with no new kit-specific rigs.

Matching transformed bearing positions and axes identifies the joint. Stud/socket connections, keyed axle connections and distinct source subassemblies identify moving members. Coaxial bearings form one rigid group. Locked stud connections, contradictory boundary axes, overlapping proposed groups, moving supports, mixed axle/pin boundaries and unknown attached receivers are rejected. Bare shafts are not exposed as movable assemblies. The batch report records supported kits and skip reasons in `studforge-motion-report.json`. Re-run `npm run studforge:motion` to update installed models without rebaking geometry.

Brick-hinge halves within 0.35 studs and with parallel axes are also considered conservative constraints, even when their placement is too approximate for the strict 0.035-stud playable match. This suppresses Pirate Roller Coaster's corner brace: its two distinct hinge axes fix the leaf. No kit-specific exclusion is used. Regression checks verify that detaching its second attachment restores motion, while approximate matches never create playable joints.

Posing changes displayed meshes only. Leaving Pose mode, entering instructions/explode/parts tray, or changing models restores the original assembly. All rigs are guarded by placement fingerprints. Detection supports this connector vocabulary, not every moving feature of a supported kit. Mixed axle/pins, clips/ball joints, gearing, sliding actuators, coupled linkages and nested independently movable groups need more shared rules. Geared mechanical groups stay disabled until linked motion is supported. Angle ranges are demonstration limits, not verified physical travel or collision checking; turntables offer ±180° and hinges/shaft pivots ±15°. Flexible-string simulation, pose persistence and edited instruction exports remain queued.

The viewer now calls this **Joints / Movable assemblies** and explicitly explains that fixed bricks stay attached. Choosing a slider highlights only its connected pieces in gold and marks the matching control; the panel shows the member count. Grabbing another supported assembly selects its control. The previous enclosing wire boxes are removed, so fixed pieces inside a box are no longer suggested as movable. Recursive picking includes printed/fixed-color faces and resolves them to their enclosing brick. Leaving joint mode restores original materials and placements. Browser checks cover Tree House slider selection/reset and helicopter slider/drag/color restoration. The Tree House's hanging basket remains fixed; no cable or basket movement was added.

Reviewed complex rigs remain available. WALL·E uses its source's annotated neck pivot. Poe's X-Wing now pivots at the four 55013 support axles through the 11458 wing bearings, rather than the engine attachment holes. Each complete engine is assigned to its own wing, including cannons/tips. Regression checks verify the bearing centers remain coaxial at 0°, 5°, 10°, 15° and 20°. The original rubber-band meshes remain static.

Validation covers source matching and changed-source rejection for every automatic rig, disjoint members, rotor blade/cap coverage, locked-joint rejection, repeated assembly instances, kit assets and all 185 preview links. Starter-preview checks ensure stacked bricks retain their model height rather than overlapping at the origin. Browser checks cover rear X-Wing opening/closing, automatic Technic controls, reset, build-step restoration and phone library layout. Physical touchscreen testing remains outstanding.

The latest batch adds Pirate Roller Coaster (31084), Mobile Crane (42108) and Crawler Crane (42042), retaining community authors and original source disclosures. All 169 kit and 16 starter previews have been regenerated with the viewer's part builder and placement conventions, including translations, rotations and mirrored parts. The software rasterizer respects hidden groups and double-sided materials, and approximates glass transmission with alpha instead of opaque surfaces. Unavailable legacy geometry is omitted from previews rather than rendered as temporary boxes; inventories and viewer behavior are preserved. `studforge-thumbnail-report.json` records each preview's coverage and unavailable part IDs. Known geometry that fails to load stops preview publication. The legacy moved-to ID repair preserves redirect rotations/translations: all six Jet Airliner tires now share their rim axes, and the same repair covers 42 placements in nine kits. The legacy converter applies the same correction on future runs.

Collapsed faces are excluded from crease-normal averaging while retaining their vertex positions and source geometry. The Mobile Crane rope contains more than 30,000 corners coincident at one point; these invisible faces previously caused quadratic smoothing. The shared viewer/preview fix completes the regression case in under one second and preserves every position. Preview PNG publication also uses the existing atomic replacement and transient Windows-lock retries.
